## Ingredients

- 1.6 kg butternut squash (1 large), halved lengthways and seeded
- 1 tbsp olive oil
- 1 onion, chopped
- 2 carrots, sliced
- 6 cloves garlic, peeled but whole
- 40 g unsalted butter
- 6 large sage leaves <!-- sage -->
- 1.4 l chicken stock, plus more to loosen
- 2 tbsp maple syrup
- 2 tbsp cider vinegar
- Cayenne
- Salt
- Crème fraîche, to serve
- Chives, chopped, to serve

## Method

1. Heat the oven to 200°C. Oil the bottom of a roasting tin, tip in the onion, carrots and garlic, salt them well and toss to coat.
2. Sit the squash halves cut side up on the vegetables and salt the cut faces. Roast for about 1 hour, until a knife goes into the flesh with no resistance. Leave to cool enough to handle.
3. Melt the butter in a small pan over medium heat. When it foams and smells nutty and the solids at the bottom are a light brown, pull it off the heat and drop in the sage leaves — they will crackle and go crisp.
4. Scoop the squash flesh into a large pot with the roasted vegetables and any juices from the tin. Add the stock, the sage butter and the maple syrup.
5. Bring to a simmer, then turn it down and leave it uncovered for 45–60 minutes. It will not look any different; it tastes rounder.
6. Blend until completely smooth, then pass it through a sieve to catch the fibrous threads. Loosen with stock if it is thicker than double cream.
7. Stir in the vinegar, then cayenne and salt a pinch at a time. Stop when it tastes sweet *and* sharp rather than only sweet — the vinegar is what keeps it from being baby food.
8. Serve with a spoon of crème fraîche and chopped chives.

Keeps four days in the fridge and freezes for three months. It thickens as it sits, so loosen it with stock on reheating.
